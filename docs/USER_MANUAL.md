# ZoomReel User Manual

This guide explains how to use ZoomReel from start to finish. It assumes no special technical knowledge. Where a technical term or abbreviation appears, it is spelled out the first time and explained.

**Current version: 1.54.** You can find the version in the small grey tag beside the word "ZoomReel" at the top left of the screen.

## 1. What ZoomReel is

ZoomReel is a drawing board with no edges. Ordinary drawing programs have a fixed sheet of paper. ZoomReel's "paper" goes on forever in every direction, and you can zoom in as far as you like. So you can draw a small picture, then zoom in and draw an even smaller one inside it, and keep going. Each zoom level is called a **depth**.

Once you have a few views you like, you save each one as a **stop**. A stop is a saved view: a particular place on the canvas at a particular zoom. When you press **Play**, ZoomReel flies the camera from stop to stop, like a film that moves through your drawing. You can also export the whole tour as a video file.

## 2. Opening the app

1. Find the file called **ZoomReel.html**.
2. Double-click it. It opens in your web browser (the program you use to surf the internet, such as Safari, Chrome or Microsoft Edge).
3. You will see a sample project called "Sample flight", with a banner across the top explaining that it is a sample.

To start your own work, press **Start a new project** in the banner, or **New project** at the top right.

> **Tip for Mac users:** Safari is the best browser for ZoomReel on a Mac, and Safari on an iPhone or iPad works too.

**A note about the internet:** the first time you open ZoomReel, it borrows its lettering (called *fonts*) from a service run by Google, and it loads a small helper for making videos. Keep your connection on for the first run. After that, most of the app keeps working without it, but exporting a video may need the helper again.

## 3. The screen at a glance

The screen has four main areas.

**The top bar** runs across the top. It shows:
- **ZoomReel** and its version number (for example *v1.54*).
- The **project name**, which you can click to change.
- A note telling you when your work was last kept in the browser.
- **New project**, which starts a fresh project.
- A moon-or-sun button for switching between dark and light mode.

**The canvas** is the large area in the middle. This is where you draw. Along the left edge of the canvas is a column of **tool buttons**. Along the right edge of the canvas are the **zoom buttons**: a plus (+) to zoom in, a minus (−) to zoom out, and buttons to turn the view.

**The control panel** runs down the right side. It has seven tabs across the top:
- **Draw**: brushes, shapes, text, the eraser and the fill tool.
- **Objects**: a list of everything you have made.
- **Stops**: your saved views and their timing.
- **Canvas**: the view controls and the background pattern.
- **Export**: making a picture or a video.
- **File**: saving, opening, keyboard shortcuts and settings.

Each tab shows its own set of controls, so you only see what you need for the job at hand.

**The timeline bar** runs along the bottom. It shows your stops as blocks in order, the total length of the tour, and the **Play** and **Present** buttons.

Along the bottom of the canvas, small labels show the current zoom (for example **×1/2.13**), the rotation (**0°**), and buttons such as **Fit all**, **Level view**, **Drawing on: Artwork**, **Text on** and **Guides off**.

![The Stops tab, showing five saved stops](images/02-stops-tab.png)

## 4. Light mode and dark mode

Press the moon-or-sun button at the top right to switch between **light mode** (dark writing on a light background) and **dark mode** (light writing on a dark background). Your choice is remembered on that computer.

Dark mode changes the control panel and the top bar. Your drawing keeps its own colours, so a white page stays white.

![The app in dark mode](images/07-dark-mode.png)

## 5. Moving around: zooming, panning and rotating

**Zooming in and out**
- On a Mac trackpad, pinch with two fingers. Hold the Command key (⌘) while scrolling with two fingers or using a mouse wheel.
- Press the **+** and **−** buttons at the right edge of the canvas.
- Use the **Zoom** slider on the **Canvas** tab. You can also type an exact number into the box beside the slider, for more precision.
- Zooming is always centred where you are looking, so you can zoom into the exact spot you want.

**Moving the view (panning)**
- Hold the **Space** bar and drag, or use the **Hand** tool (the hand icon in the tool column).
- On a trackpad, scroll with two fingers.

**Turning the view (rotating)**
- Press **,** (comma) or **.** (full stop) to turn the view 15 degrees.
- On the **Canvas** tab, use the **−15°** and **+15°** buttons, or the **Rotation** slider.
- Turning the view does not change your drawing. It only changes how you look at it.

**Useful view buttons**
- **Fit all** zooms out so everything you have made is visible at once.
- **Level view** turns the view back to straight.
- **Level view** and **Fit all** are also on the Canvas tab.

![The Canvas tab with zoom and rotation controls](images/04-canvas-tab.png)

## 6. Drawing

Open the **Draw** tab. At the top is a row of tool buttons:

- **Select**: click to choose an item. Drag around several items to choose them together.
- **Brush**: draw freehand.
- **Text**: type words onto the canvas.
- **Shapes**: squares, stars, hearts and more (see section 7).
- **Guides**: lines and markers that help you line things up. They never appear in videos or presentations.
- **Fill**: fill a closed outline with colour.
- **Eraser**: rub out marks.
- **Hand**: move the view around.

**Brush styles.** Under the tool row, choose one of six brushes:
- **Pen**: a clean, even line.
- **Marker**: a thick, soft line.
- **Pencil**: a rough, grainy line.
- **Highlighter**: a see-through wide stroke, like a highlighter pen.
- **Taper**: a line that thins out at the end.
- **Chisel**: a flat-edged stroke, like a calligraphy pen.

**Brush settings.** Choose **Fine**, **Medium** or **Thick** for a quick size. Then fine-tune with:
- **Size**: how wide the line is, in pixels (dots on the screen).
- **Opacity**: how see-through the line is. 100% is solid.
- **Smoothing**: how much the app tidies up a shaky line. Higher values give smoother lines.

**Colour.** Pick a colour from the swatches. Scroll down the panel for more.

**Pen pressure.** If you have an Apple Pencil or another pressure-sensitive stylus, tick **Pen pressure**. Pressing harder then makes a thicker line.

**Layers.** A layer is a sheet that sits on top of the others. ZoomReel has three:
- **Artwork** is your drawing. It always shows.
- **Text** holds your words. It shows in videos when it is switched on.
- **Guides** holds your helper lines. It never appears in videos or presentations.

Tick or untick a layer to hide or show it. The chip on the canvas labelled **Drawing on:** tells you which layer your next mark will go onto.

**Drawings.** Everything you draw at one zoom level is grouped into a named **drawing** (for example, "Drawing 1"). If you zoom in, even slightly, and then draw, the new marks start a new drawing. This keeps your work organised by depth.

![The Draw tab, with the brush settings and layers](images/01-editor-draw.png)

## 7. Shapes, guides and text

**Shapes.** Press the **Shapes** button, or the letter **U**, to open the shape picker. There are 21 ready-made shapes, including rectangles, triangles, stars, a heart, a cloud and a speech bubble. Click a shape, then drag on the canvas to draw it. Each shape can be an outline, filled, or both. Because shapes are ordinary drawing marks, you can colour them with **Fill**, rub bits off with the **Eraser**, or flip them.

**Guides.** Guides are helper marks: an arrow, a circle, a square and a straight line. They are useful for pointing things out while you work. Guides never appear in exported videos or presentations, so you can use them freely. Turn them on or off with **Guides off** on the canvas, or the letter **G**.

**Text.** Choose the **Text** tool, click on the canvas, and type. To change words you have typed, click them again with the Text tool.

## 8. Bringing in pictures

You can bring in pictures from your computer in three ways:
1. Press **Import images** on the **Objects** tab, then choose files.
2. Drag pictures from your Finder (the Mac's file browser) or desktop straight onto the canvas.
3. Copy a picture in another app, then paste it into ZoomReel with **Command-V** (⌘V), or press the **Paste** button on the **Objects** tab.

**Supported picture types.** ZoomReel reads the common formats:
- **PNG** (Portable Network Graphics), which can have see-through areas.
- **JPEG**, the usual format for photographs.
- **WebP**, a compact format used on the web.
- **GIF** (Graphics Interchange Format) and animated **APNG** (Animated Portable Network Graphics). Animated files keep moving in the editor.

**Trimming see-through edges.** Many PNG pictures have empty, see-through borders. Leave the box **Trim transparent edges on import** ticked, and ZoomReel cuts away the empty border, leaving a margin of about 20 pixels all round so nothing looks cramped.

**Where pictures land.** New pictures appear in the middle of your current view, at half their full width. Zoom to the depth you want before importing, or move the picture afterwards.

**Arranging several pictures at once.** If you import two or more pictures together, ZoomReel opens an **Arrange** window. Choose a layout:
- **Side by side**: a neat grid.
- **Zoom tunnel**: each picture sits inside the quiet spot of the one before, so you zoom in from one to the next.
- **Straight down**: each picture is centred inside the one before, getting smaller as it goes.
- **Cascade**: each picture is half the size of the last and steps off diagonally. There are four directions. **Random** scatters them at varied sizes, and gives a new pattern each time you press Arrange.

Tick **Add a stop for each picture** if you want a stop created for each one, named after its file. Press the arrangement button to place them. Undo (⌘Z) takes the whole arrangement back in one step.

![The Arrange window with its four layouts](images/08-arrange-dialog.png)

## 9. Objects: the list of everything you have made

The **Objects** tab lists every picture, drawing and text label in your project.

Each row has five small icons:
- **Drag handle** (the six dots on the left). Hold it and drag up or down to change the order.
- **Eye**. Click to **hide** the item, or click again to **show** it. Hidden items are left out of videos and presentations, but are not deleted.
- **Pencil**. Click to **edit** the item. Its settings open. Click the tick to **confirm** your changes and close the settings.
- **Arrow**. **Go To** moves the view straight to that item, so you can see it.
- **Bin**. **Delete** removes the item. Undo (⌘Z) brings it back.

Above the list, you can:
- **Filter** the list to show All items, just Images, just Drawings, or just Text.
- **Sort** the list by stacking order (drag to reorder), date added, name, size or zoom level.

**Selecting one item inside a drawing.** A drawing is a group of marks. To pick out one mark inside it, hold the **Option** key (⌥) and click it.

![The Objects tab, listing images, drawings and text](images/03-objects-tab.png)

## 10. Stops: saving views and building a tour

A **stop** is a saved view. Think of a stop as a bookmark that remembers where the camera was pointing and how far it was zoomed.

**Making a stop**
1. Zoom and move until the view is the one you want.
2. Go to the **Stops** tab and press **Add stop from current view**. (Or press the letter **S**.)
3. The stop appears in the list. It takes the name of the view, which you can change.

Before you add a stop, you can choose a **Frame** shape at the top of the Stops tab: **16:9** (widescreen, the usual video shape), **4:3**, **1:1** (square), **4:5** or **9:16** (tall, for phone screens). The orange corners on the canvas show the frame. Make sure your picture fits inside them.

**Each stop has these buttons:**
- **Drag handle** to change its position in the tour.
- **Eye** to hide the stop. Hidden stops are skipped during playback and video export.
- **Pencil** to open the stop's settings. Click the tick to close them.
- **Arrow** (Go To) to jump the editor to that stop.
- **Bin** to delete the stop.

**Timing settings.** In a stop's settings, you can choose:
- **Arrival time**: how many seconds the camera takes to fly to this stop.
- **Hold time**: how many seconds the camera waits on this stop before moving on.
- **Easing**: the way the camera speeds up and slows down. A gentle ease makes a smooth start and finish. A linear move keeps an even speed.

**How the camera moves.** Between stops the camera can follow one of three paths:
- **Pull back**: it zooms out, moves across, then zooms in again. This is good for showing the big picture between two details.
- **Direct** (sometimes called "travel"): it flies straight from one view to the next.
- **Arc**: it curves along a rising and falling path, like a swoop.

The timeline at the bottom shows each stop as a block, with its move drawn as a curve above it.

![The timeline at the bottom, showing stops and moves](images/02-stops-tab.png)

## 11. Playing and presenting

**Play** plays your tour inside the editor. Press the blue **Play** button below the canvas, or press the letter **P**. Press it again to pause.

Use the **step** buttons on either side of Play to move one stop forward or back. On a small phone screen, the time readout is hidden to make room.

**Present** shows the tour full screen, for showing to other people.
1. Press **Present** at the bottom right of the timeline. The screen fills with your drawing.
2. The camera glides to the first stop, then plays the whole tour.
3. The player bar (the strip of controls) is hidden to keep the view clean. Click, tap, or press **Escape** to bring it back. It fades away again after a few seconds of no activity.
4. Press **Escape** with the bar showing to stop the presentation. Press **Resume** to carry on from where you paused.

While presenting, the **Space** bar or the letter **K** plays and pauses. The arrow keys move between stops.

![Presenting full screen](images/09-presenting.png)

## 12. The Canvas tab: backdrops and the view

The **Canvas** tab sets the background behind your drawing. Choose from a set of backdrops:
- Plain colours such as **Plain**, **Warm paper** and **Studio**.
- Scenes such as **Sunset**, **Deep ocean**, **Snowfall**, **Fireflies**, **Nebula**, **Aurora** and **Rain on glass**.
- Textured papers and patterns, including maps and sheets of music.

Your backdrop is part of your project, so it is saved with it. It appears in videos and presentations but does not change your drawing.

The top of the Canvas tab holds the view controls described in section 5: **Zoom**, **Rotation**, the **−15°** and **+15°** buttons, **Level view** and **Fit all**.

## 13. Exporting: pictures and videos

Open the **Export** tab.

**Resolution** is the size of the picture in pixels. More pixels give a sharper result but a bigger file. The choices are **720p** (basic), **1080p** (full high definition, the usual choice), **1440p** (very sharp) and **4K** (the highest, sometimes called "Ultra HD"). The letter *p* stands for progressive scan, a way of drawing each frame of the picture.

**Frame rate** is how many pictures ZoomReel shows each second. The choices are **24** (the look of cinema films), **30** (the usual video rate) and **60** (very smooth motion, larger files).

**Quality** is **Standard** or **High**. High looks better but makes a bigger file.

The panel also shows you the **Output** size, the **Length** of the video, the number of **Frames**, and the **Estimated size**. The **Encoder** line tells you how the video is being made.

**Making a video (MP4).** Press **Export MP4 video**. An MP4 is the most widely accepted video file type; it plays on phones, computers and televisions. A progress window shows what stage the export is at, and a percentage. Video is made one frame at a time, so it can run slower than the video itself. Keep the ZoomReel window open and in front while it works.

If something goes wrong, the window stays on screen and shows the step where it stopped. Press **Copy details** to copy a message you can paste into an email for help.

**Saving a single picture (PNG).** Press **Save current frame as PNG**. A PNG is a still picture, useful for thumbnails or printing. It saves the view exactly as it appears in the frame.

**File names.** Exports are named in this pattern:

> **YYYY-MM-DD ZoomReel-[Project name]-[Resolution].[file type]**

For example, `2026-10-24 ZoomReel-Sample flight-1920x1080.mp4`. The first part is the date (year, month, day). The file type at the end is **mp4** for video or **png** for pictures.

## 14. Saving, opening and starting over

ZoomReel keeps your work automatically in your web browser as you go. If you close the tab and come back later, your project is still there. This is a convenience, not a permanent backup, because it lives only in that browser on that computer.

**To keep a copy you can open later, or send to someone else, use the File tab:**
- **Save project** saves a project file to your Downloads folder. Project files have the ending `.zoomreel.json`.
- **Open project…** loads a project file you saved before.
- **New project** starts a fresh, empty project. Save first if you want to keep the current one.
- **Load sample** brings back the sample project.

A JSON file (JavaScript Object Notation, a plain-text format for storing data) is simply a readable text record of your project, so keep these files just as you would a document.

**Undo and redo.** Press **⌘Z** to undo the last change and **⇧⌘Z** (Shift, Command and Z) to redo it. You can also use the undo and redo arrows in the tool column.

**Input settings.** The **File** tab also has options for how your mouse, trackpad and pen behave:
- **Mouse wheel zooms** (otherwise scrolls): turn this off if you would rather the wheel scrolled the page.
- **Rotate the view with two-finger gestures**.
- **Jumps pull back, travel, then zoom in**: how the camera moves when you jump to a stop.
- **A finger on the screen while drawing with a pen snaps to a shape**: useful on an iPad.

## 15. Keyboard shortcuts

A keyboard shortcut is a key press that does something faster than clicking. ⌘ is the Command key on a Mac, ⌥ is the Option key, ⇧ is Shift, and ⌃ is Control.

| Keys | What it does |
|---|---|
| V | Select tool |
| B | Brush |
| T | Text |
| A | Arrow guide |
| C | Circle guide |
| K | Fill (bucket) |
| E | Eraser |
| H | Hand (move the view) |
| U | Shapes |
| G | Show or hide guides |
| Space (hold) | Pan the view |
| ⌘ + scroll, or pinch | Zoom |
| , and . | Rotate the view 15° |
| ⌥ + drag or scroll | Rotate the view (Hand tool) |
| [ and ] | Make the brush smaller or bigger |
| ⌘Z | Undo |
| ⇧⌘Z | Redo |
| ⌘D | Duplicate |
| Delete | Delete the selected item |
| S | Add a stop from the current view |
| P | Play or pause |
| F | Fit everything in view |
| Left and right arrows, Page Up and Page Down | Move between stops |
| Escape | Stop presenting, or show the player bar while presenting |

## 16. Troubleshooting

**The picture or drawing looks blurry when I zoom in.**
Pictures have a fixed number of pixels. Zooming in far enough will always show their edges. Import a larger picture if you need it sharp at high zoom.

**The video export stops or says it failed.**
- Keep the ZoomReel window open and in front. Switching to another app can pause the export.
- Try a lower resolution (for example, 1080p instead of 4K) or a lower frame rate.
- Check your internet connection, because the video helper is loaded from the internet the first time.
- Press **Copy details** in the error window and keep that text if you need help.

**The sample or my work has disappeared.**
ZoomReel keeps work in the browser where you made it. Opening the app in a different browser, on a different computer, or in a private window will show a different (or empty) copy. Always use **Save project** to keep a copy you can rely on.

**Pictures import with extra empty space around them.**
Make sure **Trim transparent edges on import** is ticked on the **Objects** tab before importing.

**The text is hard to read in dark mode.**
Turn dark mode off with the moon-or-sun button, or pick a backdrop with a darker or lighter colour.

**Something on the screen is too small to tap on a phone or tablet.**
Use two fingers to zoom the whole page in Safari (on iPhone and iPad). Touch controls are made larger automatically on touch screens.

**I cannot find a control.**
Look at the tabs across the top of the control panel. Each tab has its own controls, and nothing is removed, only moved.

## 17. Words used in this guide

- **Canvas**: the large working area in the middle of the screen.
- **Depth**: how far in you have zoomed. Deeper means more zoomed in.
- **Drawing**: all the marks made at one zoom level, grouped together.
- **Easing**: the speed pattern of a camera move (fast or slow at the start and end).
- **Frame**: the box, such as 16:9, that defines the shape of a picture or video.
- **Layer**: a sheet on top of the others (Artwork, Text or Guides).
- **Stop**: a saved view that the tour visits in order.
- **APNG**: Animated Portable Network Graphics, a moving picture format.
- **FPS**: frames per second, the number of pictures shown each second.
- **GIF**: Graphics Interchange Format, a picture format that can move.
- **H.264**: a common method for squeezing video into a smaller file. ZoomReel's MP4 videos use it.
- **HTML**: HyperText Markup Language, the language web pages are written in. ZoomReel.html is a web page you can open in a browser.
- **JPEG**: a picture format often used for photographs.
- **JSON**: a plain-text format for storing project data.
- **MP4**: MPEG-4 Part 14, the most widely used video file format.
- **PNG**: Portable Network Graphics, a picture format that supports see-through areas.
- **SVG**: Scalable Vector Graphics, a picture format made of shapes and lines rather than dots. ZoomReel can work with pictures of this kind when they are imported.
- **WebP**: a compact picture format developed for the web.
